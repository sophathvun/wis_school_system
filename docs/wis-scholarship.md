# WIS Scholarship

Each scholarship exam uses a **regular academic year** as its session and name (for example, 2026–2027). Summer periods are excluded from filters, settings, candidate assignment, and exam operations. There is one new scholarship exam per regular academic year. Class rank is the student's existing rank within their campus/class/group/session; exam rank is calculated separately after marking, within each grade across participating campuses.

## Setup and use

The tab navigation opens collapsed on every page. On desktop it becomes an icon rail with label tooltips; the header button expands the labels. On phones and tablets below 992px, the header button opens the tab list and tapping outside closes it, matching Academic Reports navigation.

The Assign Student Rank form has its own header collapse/expand button. It starts expanded on computers and collapsed on phones below 768px, while the assigned-rank list remains visible. Selecting filters does not collapse the form. Crossing that responsive breakpoint updates the default; ordinary resizing within the same breakpoint does not reset a user's toggle choice.

1. **Scholarship Settings** opens saved academic years in View Only mode. Use Scholarship Academic Year to view another year, then click **Edit Settings** to enable changes. Save or Cancel returns to View Only; validation errors retain edit mode and submitted values. Confirmed-seat or published allocation, subject, and award settings stay locked. The separate **Examination Type ? Edit Title** control remains available to authorized settings staff after confirmation/publication; it changes report wording only, with a version check and an activity-log entry. **Set Up Academic Year** explicitly opens a new configuration for entry. Enter the exam date and room capacity (default 25). The academic year supplies the session name. All students assigned a class rank for that year automatically become candidates; their enrollment records supply participating campuses. There are no session-name, eligible-rank-range, campus-selection, or tie-policy settings. An existing session keeps its original academic year.
2. Enter the actual subject names. A new form offers four blank subject rows for Grades 1–6 and four for Grades 7–12. Add or remove rows as needed. Grade-specific subjects use equal From/To grades. Maximum scores and weights must be positive. Duplicate names in overlapping grade ranges are rejected.
Subject rows have Up/Down and drag controls. **Subject Exam Order**, below the subject settings, has separate default lists for Grades 1–6 and Grades 7–12. Click **Edit Exam Order** to arrange Session 1, Session 2, and so on; expand **Exam Order by Grade**, then a grade, to **Customize Order** or **Reset to Default**. Uncustomized grades follow their grade band automatically. **Save Exam Order** shows a top-right success toast; Cancel restores the last saved order. Save structural subject changes first before editing the separate order. Grade-specific subjects appear only for their eligible grades. The Attendance List A4 preview, PDF, and Excel all use each room grade's effective order, including continuations beyond four subjects. Scoring and subject-based reports use the same grade order. Authorized Settings staff may change order after confirmation/publication without reopening seats or altering subject IDs, scores, weights, papers, awards, or candidates. Updates validate exact subject permutations, check the current exam version, and record an activity event. Apply only `2026_10_16_000006_add_scholarship_subject_orders.php` for this feature; existing sessions retain their current subject order.

3. Scholarship defaults are 100% for first place, 75% for second place, 50% for third place, and 25% for each subject winner. These default percentages can be edited in settings before seats are confirmed. Tied award winners are selected manually in Result.
4. In **Assign Student Rank**, filter the regular year, campus, Grade (combined grade/class, such as 1A or 2B), and student, then choose rank **1, 2, or 3** from the compact dropdown. There is no separate Class box. The adjacent **EOY Avg.** is required, from 0 to 100 with at most two decimal places. Within the same academic year, campus, grade/class, and group, Rank 1 must have a strictly higher average than Rank 2, and Rank 2 than Rank 3. Equal or reversed averages are rejected on both new assignments and edits. Older records with no average remain preserved. Assigned students are excluded from the student dropdown for that academic year, including duplicate enrollments; use **Edit Rank** in the list to change an existing assignment. Successful saves show a small top-right SweetAlert and clear the student, rank, and average while retaining year/campus/grade filters. Blank or invalid fields show their own red border and error text, together with a SweetAlert summary. The saved average remains in the list and is restored when opening Edit Rank. Saving adds the student automatically, without a separate selection step. Ranks may be assigned before or after setting up the exam; creating or saving its settings imports all previously assigned ranks. Updating a rank or EOY average refreshes the candidate snapshot and automatically updates participating campuses. The EOY average is background academic information and is not added to scholarship exam scores. Only Grades 1–12 are supported, and a student may have only one ranked enrollment per academic year. Remove a class rank to withdraw that candidate before seats are confirmed. Campus permissions and confirmed-seat locks apply to both rank and average changes. Previously stored ranks are preserved by migrations; new assignments and edits are limited to 1–3.

   The Assigned Student Ranks list updates automatically, without an Apply Filters button or a duplicate candidate list. Selecting a year shows ranks across all permitted campuses for that year; campus, Grade, and student selections narrow the results. Campus choices come from that year's enrollments, combined grade/class choices from the selected campus, and student choices from the selected grade/class. Choices sort by grade and class order. Changing a parent clears its dependent selections. Student selection and rank entry become available after choosing a campus and Grade. The assigned-rank list sorts by campus A–Z, grade order and class A–Z, then Class Rank 1–3 before pagination. Pagination uses the Student List controls (page numbers, previous/next, entry counts, and Go to Page), defaults to 10 rows, and offers All, 10, 25, 50, 75, and 100. Page-size changes reset to page one; filter changes retain the chosen page size. All includes only rows permitted by the current filters and campus scope. Pagination keeps the current filters. Failed loads hide stale list actions; delayed responses cannot overwrite newer selections.
5. In **Scholarship Settings → Exam Centers**, add the named locations for this academic year. Campus Assignments includes only campuses with assigned student ranks in that year, with a ranked-student count. Assign each campus to one center; multiple campuses can share a center. Names must be distinct. Removing a center requires its campuses to be reassigned. A newly participating campus must also be assigned before seats can be confirmed. Settings permission for every participating campus is required. Center configuration is saved with the other exam settings and locks when seats are confirmed.
6. In **Seat Plan**, review the preview. Ordering is center order, Grade 1–12, English full name A–Z (case insensitive), then student ID and candidate ID to break name ties. Each center/grade starts a new room. Rooms never mix centers or grades. Room numbers restart at 1 in each center. Seat numbers also start at 1 in each center and continue through every room and Grade 1–12 without resetting at grade or room boundaries. The candidate identifier is displayed once as **Exam Code**, for example 2526-G01-001 for 2025-2026 and 2627-G01-001 for 2026-2027. It combines the last two digits of each year, the grade, and the A–Z candidate sequence within that grade across all centers. Grade sequences restart at 001 in each academic year. The candidate-code migration adds the prefix to existing system-generated codes while preserving imported historical and custom identifiers, student/score/award links, and room/seat assignments. The separate numeric grade-sequence column is omitted from seat lists and exports; the stored sequence remains available internally for allocation and result ordering. Center IDs and names are saved with confirmed seats and included in the list and seat exports. Confirming seats requires an explicit checkbox and a current preview token. Settings and candidate selection then lock. Historical confirmed plans without centers remain unchanged; legacy draft settings offer a Main Exam Center on their next save.
The Seat Plan list defaults to **15 records per page**, with All, 15, 25, 50, 75, and 100 options. It uses the shared Student Enrollment pagination controls, including numbered pages and Go to Page, and keeps numbering continuous across pages. Paging updates the list without reloading the form; the full plan remains covered by confirmation and exports. Phones show compact cards, and the whole form/list scrolls vertically together.

7. In **Exam Committee**, assign active user accounts as Invigilator, Score Entry, Score Reviewer, or Final Approver. Center, grade, and room scopes may be combined. Blank scopes cover all centers/grades/rooms. For center-based plans, choose a center before a room; the room must match that center and grade in the confirmed plan. Equal room numbers at different centers do not share scoring or candidate access. Historical room duties are linked to their original center by the migration. Final Approver always covers the whole exam.
8. In **Scoring**, the Datas Filter card opens expanded on desktop and collapsed on phones. Searchable filters cascade from configured Academic Year to Exam Center, Room, and Student (seat number and English name). Choices are restricted to authorized scoring/review assignments. The score list appears only after an Academic Year, Exam Center and Room are selected. Each subject header shows saved Scored and Blank counts for the current filtered list, counting zero as a score. Counts refresh after Save Draft. Its success SweetAlert lists remaining blanks only for the selected scoring subject, whose editable blank scores receive red borders and inline reminders. The selected subject is preserved after saving; other subjects remain unmarked. Entering a score, including zero, clears that reminder; incomplete drafts remain saveable. All matching students are shown without pagination; the data scrolls inside each table while both header rows remain fixed. Subject and Total Score header arrows sort each grade table numerically in either direction, keep blanks last, retain score edits, and update row numbering. Subject entry follows the displayed student order when using Tab. Grade-specific score tables show numbering, 3:4 student photos, student identity and enrollment, center/room/seat, applicable subjects in their configured grade order, and totals. One Save Draft button saves all editable students shown in the selected list, including attendance, in a single transaction. Invalid, stale, or unauthorized rows roll back the entire batch. Unchanged rows retain their reviews and score provenance. The Scoring header has a Full Screen / Exit Full Screen toggle that includes filters and score tables, hides the scholarship navigation, and retains entered values. The table uses proportional column widths to fit desktop screens and internal horizontal scrolling on narrow screens. Fullscreen allocates the remaining viewport height to student rows while keeping save/review controls visible; collapsing filters gives rows more space. Browsers without native fullscreen use an expanded viewport; Escape returns to normal view. Score inputs start locked. Select a Scoring Subject to unlock only that subject column; Tab and Shift+Tab move down and up the student rows in the active subject, with Enter also advancing. Lock Scores locks the inputs again without discarding values. Score entry validates the configured subject maximum, minimum and decimal precision before advancing or saving. Invalid fields show a red border and inline error; a SweetAlert returns focus to the score for correction, including in fullscreen. One Mark Reviewed button reviews the authorized pending students in the selected list in a single transaction with one click, without a confirmation checkbox or dialog. Incomplete, stale, or unauthorized rows roll back the entire review. Unsaved score or attendance edits disable review until the drafts are saved. Both actions preserve the selected filters. Unsaved score or attendance changes block navigation, filter changes and other form submissions with a SweetAlert offering Save Draft or Continue Editing. Canceled filter changes restore their original selections. Browser refresh/close uses the native unsaved-changes warning; successful draft submission bypasses that warning. Save score drafts; blanks remain Not Entered while zero is a valid score. An absence is separate and excludes the candidate from ranking. Previously saved scores are retained but ignored while absent. All applicable subjects must be complete, or the candidate marked absent, before a reviewer can mark the candidate Reviewed. Changing scores or attendance clears that review.
9. In **Result**, inspect totals, weighted percentages, and exam ranks. The Datas Filter card opens expanded on computers and collapsed on phones. Searchable filters cascade from configured Academic Year to Exam Center, Grade, and Seat & Student Name English; students are listed by ascending seat number. Filtering preserves original exam ranks. A selected grade narrows the winner cards while keeping the complete grade's candidate pool across centers. After all candidates in a grade are reviewed, select and save that grade's scholarship winners. Publish only when every candidate is reviewed and every grade's award decisions are saved. Published results and scores lock. Print and Excel reports are available for confirmed seating and results; result reports explicitly distinguish drafts from published results. CSV is also available for results. Reports include the selected award and scholarship percentage.

## Selecting scholarship winners

Result tables are grouped by grade and show numbering, 3:4 student photos, student identity and enrollment, exam center/room/seat/candidate number, separate sortable subject scores, sortable total score, Exam Rank, Award, and review status. The Exam Rank dropdown selects an award category for that student; the calculated score rank remains visible below it. Choosing a category fills its configured scholarship percentage (normally 100%, 75%, 50%, or 25%); authorized staff can adjust the percentage or enter a lower-score exception reason. Save Grade Winners saves the complete grade, preserving assignments outside the current center/student filter. Saving uses the exam version, shows a top-right success SweetAlert, and updates the publication version without losing edits in other grades. Duplicate categories, incomplete winner selections, and undocumented lower-score choices are blocked. Unsaved winner edits block navigation, filtering, and publication until saved. Published and historical results remain read only.

Each grade has three overall places and one award for each of its configured subjects. Four subjects therefore provide seven awards to seven distinct students, when at least seven eligible students attend. Adding a fifth subject provides eight awards.

Select the first-place winner by highest **raw total score**, then second and third place from the remaining students. Award places are separate from weighted-percentage exam ranks. When multiple students tie, choose one; ties do not automatically create extra scholarship recipients.

Next select each subject's highest-scoring remaining student, following the displayed subject order. Overall winners and earlier subject winners are excluded so each student receives only one award. The selector shows scores and the highest remaining score, including ties. If the committee chooses a lower score, enter an exception reason of at least five characters. Decisions and changes are recorded in the exam activity log. The committee may edit each winner and percentage before publication. Default percentages are starting values; a saved decision is always required.

If fewer students are eligible than available awards, fill awards in order and leave later slots vacant only once no eligible students remain. Absent, incomplete, and unreviewed students cannot win. Changing any score or attendance clears **all award decisions for that grade**; review the changed candidate and select winners again. Reopen published results with a reason before editing winners or scores. Award edits use exam version checks to reject stale forms.

Weighted percentage is `100 × sum(score / subject maximum × weight) / sum(weight)` and is displayed to four decimal places internally. Exam ranks use raw total scores separately within each grade, with fixed competition ranks (1, 2, 2, 4) for equal totals. These ranks do not automatically grant scholarships: each award slot requires one selected winner. Absent and incomplete students have no exam rank.

For 35 Grade 1 and 40 Grade 2 students with 25 per room, the rooms contain 25, 10, 25, and 15 students. Within each center, Grade 2 begins in Room 3 with Seat 36. The next center starts over at Room 1 and Seat 1. Exam Code is independent of room and seat numbering.

## Permissions and safeguards

The permission list has six independent tab grants: Assign Student Rank, Seat Plan, Scoring, Result, Exam Committee, and Scholarship Settings. Students access and WIS Scholarship View remain prerequisites. Hidden tabs reject direct URLs; rank search/filter endpoints also require the rank tab. Assign/edit/delete ranks, settings, seating, scoring/review, committee, awards and publication additionally require their action grant and matching tab. Permission toggles and saved permission lists enforce those dependencies. Exports require the corresponding Seat Plan or Result tab as well as Export access. The tab migration preserves existing rank-view and action-related tab access without granting new action rights; Super Administrator receives all tabs.

Campus staff can filter, list, assign, edit, and delete ranks only in their active assigned campuses, even if another general campus query is broader. Users assigned to an active CEN / Central / Central Office campus, global users, and users with an active global role can access all active campuses, subject to their scholarship grants. Central-scoped permission grants apply across campuses. Campus IDs and student/rank IDs supplied directly in requests are checked against the same scope; permission checks are enforced in both the controller and workflow.

Settings, seating, committee management, award selection, publication, and whole-exam exports require the corresponding permission for every participating campus. Non-super score entry/review additionally requires an explicit matching committee duty and grade/room scope. A committee assignment permits the assigned cross-campus duties without exposing unrelated candidates. Non-super publication also requires Final Approver duty.

Preview fingerprints and version checks reject stale seat plans, settings, and score forms. Candidate changes, configuration, scoring, review, committee changes, publication, and revisions have an exam activity log. Mutations lock the exam row inside database transactions to prevent concurrent publication or seat confirmation from racing with edits.

An unscored and unreviewed seat plan may be explicitly reopened with a reason, which clears its seats and room-specific committee duties. Room duties must be assigned again after confirming the replacement plan; broader grade/center duties remain. Removed room duties are recorded in the reopen activity entry. Class ranks and candidate additions/removals are frozen once seats are confirmed. Scored/reviewed plans cannot be regenerated. Published results can be explicitly reopened with a reason; corrected scores must be reviewed before republishing. Subjects, weights, and the candidate roster remain frozen once seats are confirmed.

## Future answer-sheet scanning

Only database preparation is included in the current scholarship flow; there are no answer-sheet, answer-key, camera, or automatic-marking controls yet.

`tb_scholarship_paper` reserves a configuration per subject, grade, and paper version. It contains a nullable question count, answer-choice labels, and points per question. A shared Grades 1–6 subject can therefore have different papers for Grade 1 and Grade 2. The academic year is obtained through the subject's exam. No paper configurations are inserted automatically. Existing Settings cannot replace subjects once paper configurations refer to them, protecting those references until the future paper-editing workflow exists.

Scores now record `entry_method` (`manual` for ordinary entry, `historical` for archived CSV imports), nullable `source_paper_id`, and nullable `source_reference` for a future scan-attempt reference. Existing scores remain manual and retain their values. Manual corrections record the previous source in the activity log, then clear the active scan reference and require normal review. These fields are not accepted from the current scoring form.

The next phase will add versioned answer keys, issued sheets with a unique identifier per candidate/paper, printable layouts, scan images and detected responses, confidence/review handling, and phone-camera or file-upload controls. The scanner must validate the candidate's exam, grade, and subject, calculate scores against the correct key on the server, and use the existing review, publication, and award-invalidation safeguards. The future paper editor must validate grade ranges, question counts, choices, and total points against the subject maximum before a paper can be issued. The reserved fields do not implement scanning or guarantee scan accuracy.

## Historical Import / Export

Open **Students > WIS Scholarship > Import / Export**. Set up each historical regular academic year and its actual subjects, maximum scores, and weights in Scholarship Settings first. The transfer tab lists configured years, including those without candidates. Choose a year, download **Sample CSV - 8 Records**, replace the examples with actual data, save as CSV UTF-8, then **Validate & Preview**. The subject columns are generated from that year's settings. **Sample Excel - 8 Records** provides the same template as a real `.xlsx` workbook for opening and editing in Excel; save it as **CSV UTF-8 (Comma delimited)** before import. CSV downloads include a UTF-8 BOM, Windows CRLF line endings, and an explicit `.csv` filename. If Windows does not open a CSV from the download list, open Excel and use **Data > From Text/CSV**. Without a configured year, generic eight-record samples are still available. The eight rows demonstrate `total_1` / 100%, `total_2` / 75%, `total_3` / 50%, four `subject` / 25% awards with distinct `award_subject` names, and a student with all three award fields blank. Subject examples use the first four subjects for a grade in the selected settings; if no grade has four subjects, the screen explains that only available subject examples can be populated. Class Rank stays within 1–3 across example classes A, B, and C. Totals, percentages, and tied exam ranks agree with the example scores.

Each CSV row represents one student. Required columns are `academic_year`, `student_id`, and `class_rank` (1-3). Students must already exist and have a matching enrollment in the selected historical year; current enrollments are never substituted. Campus, grade, class, and group are optional checks against that historical enrollment, and can disambiguate multiple enrollments. Names and placement are copied into historical candidate snapshots. EOY averages may be blank when unavailable. Historic class ranks and awards are preserved rather than checked against today's EOY ordering and winner-selection rules.

Optional columns cover EOY average, original exam code/center/room/seat, absence, total, percentage, exam rank, award category/subject/percentage, and `score:Subject Name` columns. Complete subject scores are validated against the original configured maximum scores and weights; a supplied mismatching total or percentage is rejected. Totals-only, awards-only, and partial subject-score archives are accepted without inventing missing values or treating blanks as zero. Room and Seat must be supplied together; missing seats are never generated automatically. `award_category` accepts `total_1`, `total_2`, `total_3`, `subject`, or the original award name; `subject` needs a configured `award_subject`. Original tied ranks, winners, and percentages are retained.

`exam_center_location` optionally restores the exam center city/province. It requires an exam center name; rows sharing a center must agree on location. Blank or absent values retain existing locations, and newly created centers without a location default to Phnom Penh. `subject_order` optionally contains portable JSON with `band` (the complete default order for the student's grade band) and `grade` (null for inheritance, or a complete grade override). Entries are subject names; repeated names across grade ranges export as `{name,grade_from,grade_to}` objects. No database subject IDs appear in CSV. Rows in the same band/grade must agree, all applicable subjects must appear exactly once, and no subject definitions, score maxima, weights, or scores are changed by this metadata. Blank or missing columns preserve saved settings, so older CSV files remain compatible. Samples (CSV and Excel) retain eight award examples and now show center locations and portable subject-order values.

Preview performs no data writes. It identifies missing/mismatched enrollments, duplicate students/seats, unknown subject columns, invalid scores, and conflicting existing records. The Shared Report Settings Changes table previews current and imported locations/defaults/overrides; these settings affect all reports for the relevant center or grade, including students outside the file. Existing archives require explicit replacement mode for shared metadata changes. No import is allowed while errors remain. The display shows up to 100 valid rows and 100 errors while validating the whole file (maximum 5 MB / 5,000 students). Import requires acknowledgement, a SweetAlert confirmation, a user-bound preview token valid for 30 minutes, and server-side revalidation under the academic-year/exam locks. Changes to the file, settings, or matched student data invalidate the preview. A successful import is atomic and records an activity-log entry.

Default mode adds new records only. **Replace matching records after preview** explicitly replaces the selected students' rank, scores, original seat information, and awards, including blank values; other students and academic years are retained. Populated report settings columns may update the shared center or grade settings shown in the preview; untouched center and grade settings remain unchanged. Imports into ordinary exams that already have confirmed seats, scores, or awards are rejected even in replacement mode. Existing draft candidates may be explicitly replaced. Imported exams are marked Historical, with importer and import timestamp, and protected from normal settings, rank, seat allocation, scoring, committee, award, and publication mutations. Authorized subsequent CSV imports provide the correction path. Report titles and print-format settings remain editable. Results and result reports use stored historical values rather than recalculating ranks. Historical imports do not pretend to have undergone the normal review/publication process.

**Export CSV** downloads the same column layout, with configured subject names, saved ranks/results, and original awards; campus access and Export permission limit the rows. UTF-8 BOM supports spreadsheet applications, and cells beginning with spreadsheet formula prefixes are escaped. The new Import / Export tab permission and Import Historical Scholarship Records action appear in Permissions. Import requires both grants and access to every existing participating campus plus each incoming student's campus. Export requires the tab and Export grants. The migration grants the new permissions only to the existing super-admin role.

Apply the additive historical import migration when deploying:

```sh
php artisan migrate --path=database/migrations/2026_10_16_000002_add_scholarship_historical_import.php --force
```

This migration adds nullable historical-import metadata and permissions. It does not import sample data or change existing scholarship results.

## Deployment

The Assigned Student Ranks Excel export uses the same report data, campus filters, pagination, branding, and saved signature settings as the A4 Print/PDF list. Its editable worksheet includes the Khmer/English heading, School Logo 1 (raster or SVG), academic year and campus scope, merged No. header with both numbering columns, yellow column headers, campus group bands, final total, and two campus or three Central signature blocks. It uses A4 portrait print settings, manual page breaks from the shared report pages, and page-number footers. Student IDs remain literal text, and user content cannot become an Excel formula. The legacy results workbook retains its existing format. No new production dependency or database migration is required.

Outstanding-student Print/PDF reports use a 65 mm School Logo 1 and show the report scope on its own line below the academic year (for example, BCH Campus or All Campuses). When signatures require an extra page, pagination carries three trailing students to that page instead of splitting the preceding list in half. Compact row and signature spacing keep the larger heading above the footer in both browser printing and PDF exports.

Print Settings uses searchable HRM staff selectors. Campus settings have two required signers: Prepared by and Approved by. Central (CEN) settings have three required signers: Prepared by, Checked and Verified by, and Approved by. The verifier fields are hidden, disabled, and excluded from validation and submission for campus settings; the server ignores any submitted campus verifier fields. Each selector includes only active staff whose primary campus or active shared assignment matches the selected settings campus: BCH settings show BCH staff, and Central's all-campus report settings show CEN staff only. Only staff ID, names, staff code, and position are exposed. The server validates selected IDs against the same campus scope and copies the HRM name and position into the saved settings. Positions display below the names in the form and report. The saved name/position snapshots remain unchanged if HRM later changes; saving the settings explicitly refreshes them. Existing manually entered names and dates survive the migration and remain printable; select their HRM records when next editing the settings to attach positions. Signature dates use the shared Student Enrollment calendar style, with year selection and a Today button, while retaining ISO dates for storage.

The gear beside the Assigned Student Ranks export buttons opens Campus Print Settings. Staff with Assign Student Rank permission can save signer names with independent dates for their permitted campus and regular academic year. Central staff can select their permitted campuses; CEN is labelled All-campus Report and is the default signature setting for a combined Central report. These settings remain editable after exam seats or results are locked, since they only format the report. Campus users can print their permitted campus data; Central can print all permitted campuses together. The combined list groups students beneath campus-name headers, repeats the current campus header on continuation pages, and prints the overall total and three signature blocks once at the end. A campus-filtered report uses that campus's settings and prints two equal signature columns, even when Central staff print it. Without saved settings, the preparer defaults to the current user, dates to today, and other signers remain blank. Both Khmer national header lines use the bundled Khmer OS Muol Light font. Existing verifier columns are nullable, so the two/three-signer behavior needs no additional migration.

## Invigilators and subject sessions

Exam Committee now contains an independent Invigilator List for the selected regular scholarship academic year. Add From HRM copies Staff ID, English name, gender, campus, position, phone and email into the yearly record; missing information can be completed before saving. No HRM foreign key or live name/phone lookup is used for saved invigilators, so HRM edits, soft deletion and permanent deletion do not remove the history. External people can be entered manually, with an optional Staff ID and organization. English name, gender, campus, position and phone are required. Each yearly record receives an Invigilator Code; Staff IDs cannot be duplicated within the same year. Editing a saved record changes the scholarship snapshot only. Existing system-user committee duties remain under System Access Assignments and retain their original authorization behavior.

In Scholarship Settings, save each subject's exam date, start time and end time, with per-grade overrides where needed. Exam Committee displays the resulting timetable read only. Session numbers follow each grade's saved subject order, so different grades may take different subjects in the same session. After confirming seats, select an invigilator, exam center, session and room. The room picker shows only that center's rooms with an applicable subject in the selected session. Apply to All Sessions assigns the person to all saved sessions for that room, atomically; to move an invigilator within the same exam center, select the person, session and new room and save again. Moving to another center requires removing the existing duty first. Multiple invigilators can cover a room. A person cannot have two room duties in the same session or overlapping timed sessions, including across centers. Schedule changes that introduce a conflict are rolled back. Reopening an unscored seat plan clears room duties and logs them, but preserves the Invigilator List and session schedule. Published/historical exams are read only. Management requires Exam Committee permission across all participating campuses; HRM choices are limited to active staff in accessible campuses, and exporting the saved roster additionally requires Export permission across the exam.

Sample Excel and Export Excel contain two worksheets: Invigilators and Room Assignments. The sample demonstrates an HRM-style Staff ID and an external invigilator. Replace sample data with real information. Keep template headers and sheet names. Invigilators uses invigilator_code, staff_id, staff_name_english, gender, campus, position, phone, email, organization, notes; Room Assignments uses invigilator_code, exam_center, session, room. Leave Room Assignments empty to import people before seating or scheduling is ready. IDs and phone numbers are literal text, preserving leading zeros. Upload .xlsx (maximum 5 MB and 5,000 combined records); formulas are rejected, and both inline and Excel shared-string values are supported. Validate & Preview checks all people/assignments in a rolled-back transaction. Nothing is retained until the user acknowledges and imports. A preview is tied to the user, academic year, exam version and a 20-minute session token. Any intervening exam changes require another preview. Replace Matching Records is required to change matching invigilator details or their existing session room; unrelated people and duties remain unchanged. Duplicate codes, Staff IDs, duplicate file duties, unknown people/centers/sessions/rooms, missing subjects and overlapping duties are rejected. Invalid imports roll back all changes.

Attendance List covers omit the Session, Subject, Invigilator Name and Phone Number table. Each room has one A4 cover, preserving the larger title/logo, 16 px metadata, 15 px rounded border and equal 8 mm margins. Print, PDF and Excel use the shared report grid. Invigilator records and session room assignments remain available in Exam Committee.

Cover Envelope reports use a 15 px rounded border, an 85 mm school logo and a bold 24 pt examination title. The exam date appears below Academic Year as `October 28, 2026`. Invigilator names and contact numbers come from the independent Exam Committee roster for the report's exam center, room and subject session, following that grade's saved subject order. Unassigned slots remain blank; additional invigilators continue on complete covers. Print, PDF and Excel share the same content.

Apply only `2026_10_16_000007_create_scholarship_invigilators.php` when deploying this addition. It creates the independent invigilator, exam-session and room-duty tables, with no change to HRM records or existing committee permissions.


### Linked invigilator sessions

Under Exam Committee > Linked Sessions, select an exam center, tick at least two saved sessions, choose one of them under Use Assignments From, and confirm Link Sessions. The selected source replaces all assignments in those sessions for that center; an empty source intentionally clears the target assignments. A center/session can belong to only one group. Unlink an existing group before changing its members. Groups are independent across centers and can contain more than two sessions. Linking and unlinking use the current exam version and existing committee permissions, and are blocked for published/historical exams.

Adding an invigilator, changing their room, or removing their duty from any member session applies to every member session of that center's group. Linked labels appear alongside the session rows. Subjects and dates/times remain specific to each session and grade. Every affected duty is checked for subject availability, valid seating and timing conflicts; failure rolls back the entire operation. Unlink Sessions keeps every current assignment and makes subsequent edits independent. Reopening the seat plan clears duties as before but retains the configured session groups for later assignments.

Excel import honors existing links. A single room row updates all linked sessions, and the preview displays those expanded assignments. Repeated linked-session rows must agree on the room; contradictory rooms are rejected rather than silently taking the last row. Replace Matching Records remains required to overwrite saved rooms. Import cannot move one session away from a group at another center; remove its linked duty first. Export writes each actual session duty, so all linked-session assignments are included. The two-sheet template remains compatible; link groups themselves are configured under Linked Sessions rather than inferred from equal imported rooms.

Apply only `2026_10_16_000008_create_scholarship_session_links.php` for this addition. Existing session schedules and room assignments start independent and are not automatically linked.


### Subject dates and grade-specific exam times

Scholarship Settings > Exam Subjects now includes Exam Date, Start Time and End Time beside each subject's existing grade range, maximum score and weight. Grades 1?6 and 7?12 keep their current default subject configuration. Subject Exam Order & Schedule provides a separate Edit Order & Schedule action, including on confirmed seat plans, without reopening seats or changing score definitions. In Customize by Grade, each subject can use Customize Date / Times independently of Customize Order. Use Default Times removes only that subject's grade timing override; resetting the order does not remove timing overrides. Duration in minutes updates from the chosen start/end times. A subject's timing follows the subject when its exam order changes.

For example, Mathematics can use 08:00?08:50 in the primary band and 08:00?09:50 in the secondary band. Chemistry can retain one Grades 7?12 subject row, with default 90-minute timing and individual Grade 10, 11 and 12 overrides of 110 minutes. Dates can also vary by grade. No grade-range restructuring or subject duplication is required for timing customization.

Exam Committee no longer edits a shared global session timetable. Session identities and linked groups still organize duties, but room pickers, the read-only timetable and duty details use the actual subject schedule for the room's grade. Sessions are created automatically when subject schedules are saved. Dates/start/end can remain blank while preparing settings; assigning a duty requires a complete schedule for the subject in every affected session. End time must be after start time. Overlap checks use the grade's actual start/end times and date, including different durations across rooms and linked sessions. Changes to order/timing that conflict with saved duties roll back atomically. Excel duty imports use the same effective schedules; their template columns remain unchanged. Published and historical schedules remain read only.

Apply only `2026_10_16_000009_add_scholarship_subject_schedules.php`. It preserves prior session dates/times by mapping them through each grade's saved subject order, using subject defaults where possible and grade exceptions where necessary. It retains existing invigilator duties and linked-session IDs. Schedules are independent of scores and award calculations. The main exam date continues to provide report headings, while per-subject dates determine session duties.


Subject schedule date fields now use the Student Enrollment premium calendar (month navigation, year selection and Today) and display DD-MMM-YYYY, for example 20-Oct-2026. Manual date entry is also supported; submitted dates remain ISO YYYY-MM-DD. Start/end fields use editable premium clock controls with 24-hour hour/minute selectors, Clear and Done. Users can type HH:mm, compact 0830, or 8:30 AM / 2:30 PM; values normalize to HH:mm. Invalid dates/times prevent saving. The controls apply to default subject rows, dynamically added rows and per-grade timing overrides. Popups remain inside the viewport and escape scrolling tables without clipping; controls and popup listeners are cleaned up when grade lists rerender or subject rows are removed. Existing date pickers keep their previous behavior. No database migration is needed for these input controls.
