## Open and filter results

1. Open **G9 National Exams → Exam Result**.
2. Select the academic year.
3. Choose available exam center, campus, room, grade, student, or result filters.
4. Search by student ID or name when needed.
5. Check the summary totals before interpreting a graph.

The summary reflects all matching candidates, not only the current table page. A graph or statistics tab may require additional permission.

## Read the result table

The table shows candidate identity, exam details, EOY average, total score, result, and letter grade. An absent candidate keeps the Absent status in this screen. A pending result indicates required information needs review.

## Passed/Failed Graph

The graph tab includes an outcome overview and comparisons by campus or exam center.

**Letter Grade by Exam Center** shows grade counts and percentages for each center. Percentages use attended candidates as the denominator. Absent students are excluded; present candidates with pending results remain in the attendance total.

**Passed / Failed by Subject** displays both **English Exam** and **Khmer Exam** in every subject card. No language tab is needed. Green bars represent passed marks and red bars represent failed marks; each shows a count and percentage.

Subject graphs use a pass mark of **50% of the subject maximum**. Only present candidates with an entered eligible score are counted. Missing marks and absent candidates are excluded. Foreign Language is not applicable to English examinations.

Subject pass percentages describe individual marks. They are different from a student's overall examination result.

## Passed/Failed Statistics

Choose the available campus or exam-center grouping. Female counts appear below totals. The pass-rate calculation shown on this screen excludes pending and absent outcomes from the completed-result denominator.

## Why totals can differ

Reports have different purposes and denominators. Room and WIS reports include absent candidates in their failed group, while attendance remains recorded separately. Subject graphs exclude absences and missing marks. Read the labels and guide for the report you are using before comparing figures.
