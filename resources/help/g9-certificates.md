## Before you start

Open **G9 National Exams → G9 Certificate Tracking**. Viewing documents and managing tracking records have separate permissions.

## Find the correct student

1. Select the academic year and filters.
2. Search for the student using the student ID or name.
3. Verify the student ID, Khmer name, exam center, room, seat, and result.
4. Check whether the record already has a certificate number or imported history.

Only passed students are eligible. Failed, absent, and pending students cannot be issued a certificate or have a certificate uploaded through tracking.

## Upload a provisional certificate

Open the student's available certificate action. Review the number and dates, choose the certificate image, and save using **Save Certificate**. Follow the upload validation messages. The normal image upload process converts stored images to WebP.

G9 tracking has two document stages: **Provisional Certificate** and **Certificate received by student**. Review the existing document before replacing it.

## Record receipt by a student

1. Open the receipt or received-certificate action for the student.
2. Record **Student Received Date** and **Received By**.
3. Attach the received-certificate evidence where required by the form.
4. Save and check the tracking row and history.

The visible received date uses a format such as **13-Jan-2027**. Certificate dates use Khmer text, such as **០១ ធ្នូ ២០២៦**.

## Correct a received record

Corrections may require separate permission and a **Correction Reason**. Check the history before changing an already received record. Preserve evidence of the original issuance.

## Review historical imports

Imported records can contain source filenames, dates, recipient details, and review notes. Open the student's history or review information when available. A missing source photo means the backup did not contain a matching file; it does not justify generating a new certificate number.

Administrative import audit files are private server files, not a public download. Ask the authorized administrator to review unresolved matches or missing source files.

Previously issued certificate numbers are important official history. Do not replace them merely to match a new numbering format.
